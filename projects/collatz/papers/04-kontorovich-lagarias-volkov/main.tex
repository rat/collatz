\begin{abstract}
% DRAFT -- rewrite by hand before submission (Rule 4b: abstracts carry
% the highest marker density of any section in the paper).
Kontorovich and Lagarias predict a counting exponent $\eta\approx
0.650919$ for the reverse tree of the accelerated $5x+1$ map, governing
$N_u(x)=x^{\eta+o(1)}$; a competing model due to Volkov predicts
$\eta^\ast\approx0.678$, and the two authors write that Volkov's own
data cannot discriminate between the values. We report an exact
enumeration with Aitken-extrapolated truncation correction that reaches
$0.639$, a fixed-window reading that excludes neither value on its own.
Applied to a branching process of known exponent, the same estimator
under-reads by $0.038$, more than the gap between the two predictions,
so this raw reading was never evidence for either side. Building
synthetic controls that share the arithmetic tree's branching and
sibling-spacing law but draw the branch residue rather than using the
true one, via an exact sibling congruence, gives three independent
processes of exponent $0.650919$ that read within $0.004$ of each other
at a depth where the bias has fallen below $0.003$; the arithmetic tree
reads inside that band, and a control of exponent $0.678$ reads more
than seven band-widths away. The result is a measurement with
calibrated controls, not a proof, and it targets the disputed exponent
value; whether it bears on Volkov's branching model itself is a
separate question.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Collatz, or $3x+1$, conjecture asks whether every positive integer
eventually reaches $1$ under the map $n\mapsto n/2$ ($n$ even),
$n\mapsto3n+1$ ($n$ odd). Kontorovich and Lagarias
\cite{KontorovichLagarias2009} study the $3x+1$ and $5x+1$ problems
through the counting statistics of the associated reverse tree: for a
fixed root $u$, the number $N_u(x)$ of integers up to $x$ that
eventually map to $u$ satisfies $N_u(x)=x^{\eta+o(1)}$ for a conjectural
exponent $\eta$. Their stochastic model predicts $\eta_{5,BP}\approx
0.650919$ for $q=5$ (their Theorem~8.10); a competing branching model
due to Volkov, discussed in the same reference, predicts a different
value $\eta^\ast_{5,BP}\approx0.678$. Kontorovich and Lagarias write
that ``the empirical data Volkov presents seems insufficient to
discriminate between these two predicted exponents,'' and separately
note ``some controversy concerning the conjectured value of the
constant.''

\S\ref{sec:setup} fixes the reverse-tree construction and an exact
sibling congruence used later to build calibrated controls.
\S\ref{sec:dispute} describes Volkov's model and the numerical gap
$\Delta=0.027$ between the two predictions. \S\ref{sec:method}--
\S\ref{sec:result} report a direct exact enumeration, reaching a
fixed-window reading of $0.639$ that excludes neither prediction on its
own. \S\ref{sec:calibrated} shows this estimator is itself biased, by
more than $\Delta$, on a process of \emph{known} exponent, and then
builds matched synthetic controls that isolate and calibrate out that
bias, separating the arithmetic tree's reading from the $0.678$
control.

\section{Setup: the reverse tree of the accelerated $qx+1$ map}
\label{sec:setup}

Fix an odd integer $q\ge3$. The \emph{accelerated $qx+1$ map} is
\[
  T_q(n) = \frac{qn+1}{2^{v_2(qn+1)}}, \qquad n \text{ odd},
\]
where $v_2$ is the $2$-adic valuation. The \emph{reverse tree} rooted
at an odd integer $u$ coprime to $q$ is built by, at each odd node
$u$, adjoining a child $w_a := (2^a u - 1)/q$ for every $a\ge1$ such
that $2^a u\equiv1\pmod q$; such $w_a$ is automatically an odd integer
whenever it is an integer. Writing $d:=\ord_q(2)$, admissibility of
$a$ for a given parent $u$ depends only on $u\bmod q$: if some
$a_0(u)\in\{1,\dots,d\}$ satisfies $2^{a_0(u)}u\equiv1\pmod q$, it is
unique, and the admissible exponents are exactly $a\equiv a_0(u)\pmod
d$; nodes whose residue admits no such $a_0$ are sterile. At $q=5$,
$d=4$, every nonzero residue admits an $a_0$, and only $u\equiv0\pmod5$
is sterile.

We count distinct integer labels reached, ignoring repeated visits
along different paths:
\[
 N_u(x):=\#\{n\le x:n\text{ odd and }T_q^k(n)=u
                    \text{ for some }k\ge0\},
 \qquad
 N_u(x)=x^{\eta+o(1)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Sibling congruence]
\label{lem:sibling-congruence}
Let $q\ge3$ be odd, $d=\ord_q(2)$, and $u$ odd with $2^au\equiv1\pmod
q$ solvable. Write the children of $u$, in order of increasing
admissible exponent, as $w_{(j)}:=w_{a_0+jd}=(2^{a_0+jd}u-1)/q$ for
$j\ge0$, where $a_0=a_0(u)$. Then
\[
  w_{(j+1)} = 2^d w_{(j)} + \frac{2^d-1}{q} \qquad\text{(exact identity
  in $\mathbb Z$)},
\]
and consequently $w_{(j+1)}\equiv w_{(j)}+c\pmod q$, with
$c:=\bigl((2^d-1)/q\bigr)\bmod q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution: $2^d w_{(j)}+(2^d-1)/q =
\bigl(2^{a_0+(j+1)d}u-2^d+2^d-1\bigr)/q = w_{(j+1)}$. The congruence
follows from $2^d\equiv1\pmod q$.
\end{proof}

When $q$ is prime, $c=0$ would force $q^2\mid2^d-1$, the base-$2$
Wieferich condition; the only known Wieferich primes are $1093$ and
$3511$, so $c\ne0$ for every $q$ of interest here, and consecutive
siblings' residues mod $q$ advance by a fixed nonzero step. At $q=5$,
$d=4$, $c=3$. The recursion $w_{(j+1)}=2^dw_{(j)}+(2^d-1)/q$ makes
sense verbatim over $\mathbb R$ once $q$ is replaced by a real value;
\S\ref{sec:calibrated} uses exactly this to build a branching process
with the arithmetic tree's sibling spacing but a tunable exponent.

\section{The dispute}
\label{sec:dispute}

Volkov's competing model, as Kontorovich and Lagarias describe it, is a
complete binary branching process with a different encoding of the
iterates than the arithmetic reverse tree of \S\ref{sec:setup}; it
predicts $\eta^\ast_{5,BP}\approx0.678$, against the $\eta_{5,BP}\approx
0.650919$ of their own stochastic model (their Remark following
Theorem~8.10). The two values differ by $\Delta=0.027$, small enough
that the numerical evidence available to Kontorovich and Lagarias did
not settle which is closer to the truth.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We implemented the exact reverse tree of $T_5$ from scratch. A pilot
run and an initial production run of $n=300$ roots both showed the
standard failure mode of a biased estimator: once the sampling standard
error falls below the residual truncation bias, a naive confidence
interval excludes every candidate value, including the correct one. We
therefore rewrote the enumeration to track, in a single pass, the
running path-maximum at every node, giving simultaneous counts at
truncation buffers $10^9$ through $10^{13}$ (validated byte-for-byte
against the earlier buffer-by-buffer method on five test roots). With
roots restricted well below the measurement window, the pooled mean
slope across $300$ roots decays geometrically with truncation:
\[
  0.60049\ (10^9) \to 0.62387\ (10^{10}) \to 0.63261\ (10^{11}) \to
  0.63650\ (10^{12}) \to 0.63801\ (10^{13}),
\]
with increments shrinking by a factor of $0.37$ to $0.45$ per decade of
truncation, the signature of an extrapolable truncation bias rather
than sampling noise.

\section{Result: a fixed-window estimator}
\label{sec:result}

Aitken's $\Delta^2$ extrapolation of this sequence to infinite
truncation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-result}
  \hat\eta_5 = 0.639, \qquad 95\%\ \text{bootstrap interval, fixed window} =
  [0.633,\, 0.645].
\end{equation}
The bootstrap interval measures variation between sampled roots after
the truncation extrapolation; it does not include the remaining bias
from the fixed measurement window, and so cannot exclude either
asymptotic prediction. That remaining bias is visible directly: the
slope-per-decade sequence \emph{within} the fixed measurement window
($10^4\!\to\!10^5$ through $10^7\!\to\!10^8$) is still rising at the
last decade tested, $0.6021\to0.6296\to0.6432\to0.6460$, with no
plateau.

\begin{empirical}[Finite-window comparison of two exponent predictions]
\label{thm:kl}
The fixed-window estimator of the $5x+1$ reverse-tree counting
exponent, computed by exact enumeration with
Aitken-$\Delta^2$-extrapolated truncation correction, is $0.639$
(bootstrap interval $[0.633,0.645]$). The sequence of local slopes
rises monotonically toward the Kontorovich--Lagarias prediction
$0.650919$ on the available windows without stabilizing there, so the
experiment does not provide a calibrated confidence interval for the
asymptotic exponent and does not exclude the Volkov prediction.
\end{empirical}

\section{A calibrated comparison}
\label{sec:calibrated}

The estimator behind Empirical Result~\ref{thm:kl} had never been run
on a process with a known exponent. It is biased: on a branching random
walk with the arithmetic tree's own offspring law but an independently
drawn branch residue at every node (exponent provably $0.650919$, the
Kontorovich--Lagarias prediction itself, realized as a pressure-equation
root in a companion paper on the $qx+1$ branching structure
\cite{PressureCompanion}), the same window, truncation, and
extrapolation reads $0.6131$, an under-read of $0.038$, larger than the
gap $\Delta=0.027$ separating the two disputed values. A raw reading of
this estimator therefore settles nothing on its own.

The bias falls with depth, and depends on how much a process
fluctuates, so it can be calibrated out by comparing readings at a
common depth: run the
same estimator, at the same depth, on synthetic processes built to have
exponent $0.650919$ and exponent $0.678$, sharing the arithmetic tree's
offspring law and, via Lemma~\ref{lem:sibling-congruence}, its sibling
spacing, but drawing the branch residue instead of using the true one,
and see which reading the arithmetic tree matches. Replacing the
integer denominator $q$ in the lemma's recursion by a tunable real
value $q_{\mathrm{val}}$ moves the resulting process's exponent to any
target solving $q_{\mathrm{val}}^{\alpha}=q(2^{\alpha}-1)$, while
leaving the branching law and sibling spacing unchanged.

\begin{empirical}[Calibrated separation from the Volkov exponent]
\label{thm:kl-calibrated}
At the checkpoint decade $10^9\!\to\!10^{10}$, where every control
reads its own target exponent to within $0.003$: three independent
constructions of exponent $0.650919$ (the arithmetic tree's offspring
law with drawn residues, its real-valued relaxation, and an
unconstrained i.i.d.\ branching walk) read $0.64751$, $0.64981$, and
$0.65122$, a band $0.0037$ wide reflecting how much residual bias still
depends on each process's own fluctuation. The arithmetic tree reads
$0.64926$, inside that band. A construction of exponent $0.678$ reads
$0.67748$, $0.0282$ away from the arithmetic reading, more than seven
band-widths and well outside the arithmetic tree's own $95\%$ interval
$[0.64818,0.65027]$. The separation grows as bias
falls with depth: at the shallower decade $10^7\!\to\!10^8$ the same
gap is only $0.0229$. This measurement targets the exponent value
$0.678$; Volkov's branching model itself uses a different encoding of
the iterates and is not implemented here.
\end{empirical}

Pushed further on the arithmetic tree alone, past the point where the
drawn-residue controls develop a heavy tail that stalls their own
extrapolation, the slope keeps closing on $0.650919$: $0.6465$ at
$10^7\!\to\!10^8$, $0.6487$, $0.6490$, then $0.6506$ and $0.6505$ at
$10^{10}\!\to\!10^{11}$ and $10^{11}\!\to\!10^{12}$, each within
$0.0004$ of the prediction. These bootstrap bands cover root
resampling only; the systematics below are a separate term.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Two systematics bound on Empirical Result~\ref{thm:kl-calibrated}. The
drawn-residue construction and its real-valued relaxation at exponent
$0.650919$ agree to $0.0035$ over six seeds, bounding any
implementation systematic between the two recursions at about $0.004$,
comparable to the band width itself. The exponent-$0.678$ control
fluctuates more than the arithmetic tree does, which would if anything
push its own reading \emph{down}, toward the arithmetic reading; the
separation survives that direction of bias. Both systematics are
smaller than, or comparable to, the $0.0037$ band width used to read
the comparison, and an order of magnitude below the $0.0282$
separation itself.

The $0.038$ bias of \S\ref{sec:calibrated} and the $0.003$ residual
here are the same estimator at different depths. The bias shrinks by
roughly an order of magnitude between the window used for the first
measurement and the deeper checkpoint decade used for the second, and
that shrinkage is what turns an uninformative raw reading into a
comparison precise enough to separate the two disputed values.

This is a measurement with calibrated controls. It settles the
narrower question of whether the arithmetic tree's counting exponent is
closer to $0.650919$ or to $0.678$; it does not implement or test
Volkov's branching model directly, which encodes the iterates
differently from the matched controls built here. Two observations
outside this paper's scope are worth recording for future work: the
i.i.d.\ control above is Kontorovich and Lagarias's own stochastic
model, so the gap between its bias ($0.038$) and the arithmetic tree's
($0.013$ at the same depth) is a direct measurement of how much less
the arithmetic tree fluctuates than the model built to describe it; and
the deep arithmetic-only run above sits systematically at or just below
$0.650919$, a pattern the data here cannot yet distinguish from a
slowly converging extrapolation.
