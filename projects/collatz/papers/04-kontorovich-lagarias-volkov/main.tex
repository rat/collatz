\begin{abstract}
Kontorovich and Lagarias predict a counting exponent $\eta\approx
0.650919$ for the reverse tree of the accelerated $5x+1$ map, where
$N_u(x)=x^{\eta+o(1)}$. A competing model due to Volkov predicts
$\eta^\ast\approx0.678$. The two authors write that Volkov's own data
cannot discriminate between the values.

We enumerate the reverse tree exactly and extrapolate the truncation
bias with Aitken's $\Delta^2$. The fixed-window reading is $0.639$; it
excludes neither prediction. Applied to a branching process of known
exponent, the same estimator under-reads by $0.038$, more than the gap
$\Delta=0.027$ between the two disputed values, so a raw reading
settles nothing.

We build synthetic controls that share the arithmetic tree's branching
law: two also share, via an exact sibling congruence, its sibling
spacing, drawing the branch residue instead of the true one; a third
draws every branch label independently, with no such congruence. Three
such controls of exponent $0.650919$ read within $0.0035$ of that value
and within $0.004$ of each other. The arithmetic tree reads $0.64926$,
below the two congruence-matched controls and inside the wider band set
by the third. A control of exponent $0.678$ reads $0.0282$ away, more
than seven widths of that band outside it.

The comparison is a measurement, not a proof, calibrated against
controls of known exponent. It targets the disputed exponent value;
whether it bears on Volkov's branching model itself is a separate
question.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Kontorovich and Lagarias \cite{KontorovichLagarias2009} study the
$3x+1$ and $5x+1$ Collatz-type problems through the counting statistics
of the associated reverse tree: for a fixed root $u$, the number
$N_u(x)$ of integers up to $x$ that eventually map to $u$ under the
accelerated map satisfies $N_u(x)=x^{\eta+o(1)}$ for a conjectural
exponent $\eta$. Their stochastic model predicts $\eta_{5,BP}\approx
0.650919$ for $q=5$ (their Theorem~8.10, subscript $BP$ for branching
process, in their own notation); a competing branching model due to
Volkov, discussed in the same reference, predicts a different value
$\eta^\ast_{5,BP}\approx0.678$. Kontorovich and Lagarias write that
``the empirical data Volkov presents seems insufficient to discriminate
between these two predicted exponents,'' and separately note ``some
controversy concerning the conjectured value of the constant.''

\section{Setup: the reverse tree of the accelerated $qx+1$ map}
\label{sec:setup}

Fix an odd integer $q\ge3$. The \emph{accelerated $qx+1$ map} is
\[
  T_q(n) = \frac{qn+1}{2^{v_2(qn+1)}}, \qquad n \text{ odd},
\]
where $v_2$ is the $2$-adic valuation. The \emph{reverse tree} rooted
at an odd integer $u$ coprime to $q$ is built by, at each odd node
$v$, adjoining a child $w_a := (2^a v - 1)/q$ for every $a\ge1$ such
that $2^a v\equiv1\pmod q$; such $w_a$ is automatically an odd integer
whenever it is an integer. Writing $d:=\ord_q(2)$, admissibility of
$a$ for a given parent $v$ depends only on $v\bmod q$: if some
$a_0(v)\in\{1,\dots,d\}$ satisfies $2^{a_0(v)}v\equiv1\pmod q$, it is
unique, and the admissible exponents are exactly $a\equiv a_0(v)\pmod
d$; nodes whose residue admits no such $a_0$ are sterile. At $q=5$,
$d=4$, every nonzero residue admits an $a_0$, and only $v\equiv0\pmod5$
is sterile.

Every node has exactly one parent under $T_q$, so a given integer
reaches $u$, if at all, along a single path. We count them:
\[
 N_u(x):=\#\{n\le x:n\text{ odd and }T_q^k(n)=u
                    \text{ for some }k\ge0\},
 \qquad
 N_u(x)=x^{\eta+o(1)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Sibling congruence]
\label{lem:sibling-congruence}
Let $q\ge3$ be odd, $d=\ord_q(2)$, and $v$ odd with $2^av\equiv1\pmod
q$ solvable. Write the children of $v$, in order of increasing
admissible exponent, as $w_{(j)}:=w_{a_0+jd}=(2^{a_0+jd}v-1)/q$ for
$j\ge0$, where $a_0=a_0(v)$. Then
\[
  w_{(j+1)} = 2^d w_{(j)} + \frac{2^d-1}{q} \qquad\text{(exact identity
  in $\mathbb Z$)},
\]
and consequently $w_{(j+1)}\equiv w_{(j)}+c\pmod q$, with
$c:=\bigl((2^d-1)/q\bigr)\bmod q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution: $2^d w_{(j)}+(2^d-1)/q =
\bigl(2^{a_0+(j+1)d}v-2^d+2^d-1\bigr)/q = w_{(j+1)}$. The congruence
follows from $2^d\equiv1\pmod q$.
\end{proof}

When $q$ is prime, $c=0$ would force $q^2\mid2^d-1$, the base-$2$
Wieferich condition; the only known Wieferich primes are $1093$ and
$3511$, so $c\ne0$ for every $q$ of interest here, and consecutive
siblings' residues mod $q$ advance by a fixed nonzero step. At $q=5$,
$d=4$, $c=3$. Dropping the additive constant, negligible once $w_{(j)}$
is large, leaves the multiplicative step $w_{(j+1)}\approx2^dw_{(j)}/q$
together with the residue step $c$; both extend to any real denominator
$q_{\mathrm{val}}$ in place of $q$. \S\ref{sec:calibrated} uses exactly
this to build a branching process with the arithmetic tree's sibling
spacing but a tunable exponent.

\section{The dispute}
\label{sec:dispute}

Volkov's competing model, as Kontorovich and Lagarias describe it, is a
complete binary branching process with a different encoding of the
iterates than the arithmetic reverse tree of \S\ref{sec:setup}; it
predicts $\eta^\ast_{5,BP}\approx0.678$, against the $\eta_{5,BP}\approx
0.650919$ of their own stochastic model (their Remark following
Theorem~8.10). The two values differ by $\Delta=0.027$. Kontorovich and
Lagarias write that the data Volkov presents cannot settle which of the
two is closer to the truth.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We enumerate the exact reverse tree of $T_5$ from $300$ roots: odd
integers coprime to $5$, sampled uniformly below $10^4$ and outside
every cycle of $T_5$, so the entire measurement window lies beyond
them. A single depth-first pass tracks the running path-maximum at
every node, giving simultaneous counts at truncation buffers $10^9$
through $10^{13}$; this agrees, count for count, with an independent
buffer-by-buffer implementation on five held-out roots. A naive
confidence interval built from a single fixed buffer excludes every
candidate exponent, including the correct one, once the sampling
standard error falls below the residual truncation bias; the
path-maximum construction sidesteps this by extrapolating the buffer
dependence directly. The pooled mean slope across the $300$ roots
decays geometrically with truncation:
\[
  0.60049\ (10^9) \to 0.62387\ (10^{10}) \to 0.63261\ (10^{11}) \to
  0.63650\ (10^{12}) \to 0.63801\ (10^{13}),
\]
with increments shrinking by a factor of $0.37$ to $0.45$ per decade of
truncation: the signature of an extrapolable truncation bias.

\section{Result: a fixed-window estimator}
\label{sec:result}

Aitken's $\Delta^2$ extrapolation of this sequence to infinite
truncation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-result}
  \hat\eta_5 = 0.639, \qquad 95\%\ \text{bootstrap interval, fixed window} =
  [0.633,\, 0.645].
\end{equation}
The bootstrap interval measures variation between sampled roots after
the truncation extrapolation; it does not include the remaining bias
from the fixed measurement window, and so cannot exclude either
asymptotic prediction. That remaining bias is visible directly in a
diagnostic panel: on $40$ of the $300$ roots, at the deepest buffer
$10^{13}$, the per-decade slope across $10^4\!\to\!10^5$ through
$10^7\!\to\!10^8$ is still rising at the last decade tested,
$0.6021\to0.6296\to0.6432\to0.6460$, with no plateau.

\begin{empirical}[Finite-window reading of the counting exponent]
\label{thm:kl}
The fixed-window estimator of the $5x+1$ reverse-tree counting
exponent, computed by exact enumeration with
Aitken-$\Delta^2$-extrapolated truncation correction, is $0.639$
(bootstrap interval $[0.633,0.645]$). The sequence of local slopes
rises monotonically across the available windows, still increasing at
the last one tested; \S\ref{sec:calibrated} extrapolates the same
estimator substantially deeper. On its own, this experiment does not
provide a calibrated confidence interval for the asymptotic exponent
and does not exclude the Volkov prediction.
\end{empirical}

\section{A calibrated comparison}
\label{sec:calibrated}

The estimator behind Empirical Result~\ref{thm:kl} is biased: on a
branching random walk with the arithmetic tree's own offspring law but
an independently drawn branch residue at every node, exponent provably
the Kontorovich--Lagarias value $0.650919$ itself, realized as a
pressure-equation root in a companion paper on the $qx+1$ branching
structure \cite{PressureCompanion}, the same window, truncation, and
extrapolation read $0.6131$. That is an under-read of $0.038$, larger
than the gap $\Delta=0.027$ separating the two disputed values.

The bias falls with depth, and depends on how much a process
fluctuates, so it can be calibrated out by comparing readings at a
common depth. Two constructions share the arithmetic tree's offspring
law and, via Lemma~\ref{lem:sibling-congruence}, its sibling spacing,
with a drawn branch residue in place of the endogenous one: one built
to exponent $0.650919$, one to $0.678$. Replacing the integer denominator
$q$ in the lemma's multiplicative step by a tunable real value
$q_{\mathrm{val}}$ moves the resulting construction's exponent $\alpha$
to any target solving $q_{\mathrm{val}}^{\alpha}=q(2^{\alpha}-1)$, with
$q$ itself held fixed at the arithmetic tree's own admissibility rule
and sterility probability. A third construction, the drawn-residue
walk of the paragraph above, shares only the offspring law and draws
every branch label independently, with no sibling congruence; it
bounds how much of the calibration depends on that structure. Every
construction below runs on the same $300$ roots as \S\ref{sec:method},
at checkpoints $10^4$ through $10^{10}$ and truncation buffers $10^9$
through $10^{15}$, and the arithmetic tree's reading is checked against
them.

\begin{empirical}[Calibrated separation from the Volkov exponent]
\label{thm:kl-calibrated}
At the checkpoint decade $10^9\!\to\!10^{10}$, the two congruence-matched
constructions of exponent $0.650919$ read $0.65122$ (drawn residue,
sibling congruence) and $0.64981$ (its real-valued relaxation), a band
$0.00141$ wide. The unconstrained construction of the same exponent
reads $0.64751$, $0.0035$ below target; including it widens the band to
$0.0037$. The arithmetic tree reads $0.64926$, below both
congruence-matched constructions and inside the wider band set by the
unconstrained one. A construction of exponent $0.678$ reads $0.67748$,
$0.0282$ away from the arithmetic reading, more than seven widths of
that wider band. The same comparison at the shallower decade
$10^7\!\to\!10^8$, on the same checkpoint grid, gives an arithmetic
reading of $0.64622$ against $0.67200$ for the exponent-$0.678$
construction, a gap of $0.0258$: the separation grows with depth. This
measurement targets the exponent value $0.678$; Volkov's branching
model itself uses a different encoding of the iterates and is not
implemented here.
\end{empirical}

Pushed further on the arithmetic tree alone, to checkpoints $10^{12}$
and buffers $10^{17}$, past the point where the drawn-residue
constructions develop a heavy tail that stalls their own extrapolation,
the slope keeps closing on $0.650919$: $0.6465$ at $10^7\!\to\!10^8$,
$0.6487$ at $10^8\!\to\!10^9$, $0.6490$ at $10^9\!\to\!10^{10}$, then
$0.6506$ and $0.6505$ at $10^{10}\!\to\!10^{11}$ and
$10^{11}\!\to\!10^{12}$, the last two within $0.0005$ of the
prediction. This deeper grid's $0.6490$ at $10^9\!\to\!10^{10}$ is
close to, but computed on different checkpoint and buffer values than,
the $0.64926$ of Empirical Result~\ref{thm:kl-calibrated}. The
bootstrap bands on these five readings cover root resampling only; a
separate truncation term, bounded above by $0.002$ (by $0.0003$ to
$0.0004$ from $10^8\!\to\!10^9$ onward), is a systematic addressed
next.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Two systematics bound Empirical Result~\ref{thm:kl-calibrated}. The
drawn-residue construction and its real-valued relaxation at exponent
$0.650919$ agree to $0.0035$ over six seeds, bounding any
implementation systematic between the two recursions at about $0.004$,
comparable to the wider band width itself. The exponent-$0.678$
construction fluctuates more than the arithmetic tree and carries a
correspondingly larger window-estimator bias, $0.019$ against $0.011$;
that pulls its reading down, toward the arithmetic reading, so the
separation survives that direction of bias. Both systematics are
smaller than, or comparable to, the $0.0037$ wider band width used to
read the comparison, and well below the $0.0282$ separation itself.

The $0.038$ bias of the opening measurement in \S\ref{sec:calibrated}
and the residual bias at the calibration depth, under $0.004$ for every
construction there, are the same estimator at different depths. The
bias falls by roughly an order of magnitude between the window used
for the first measurement and the deeper checkpoint decade used for the
second.

The comparison settles the narrower question of whether the arithmetic
tree's counting exponent is closer to $0.650919$ or to $0.678$. It does
not implement or test Volkov's branching model directly, which encodes
the iterates differently from the matched constructions built here. The
unconstrained construction is Kontorovich and Lagarias's own stochastic
model: accelerating their branching process collapses each run of
doublings into a single jump of ratio $2^a/5$ with $a$ drawn uniformly
from $\{1,2,3,4\}$, exactly the drawn-label walk used here. The gap
between its bias ($0.038$) and the arithmetic tree's ($0.012$ at the
same depth) measures how much less the arithmetic tree fluctuates than
the model built to describe it, consistent with the arithmetic tree
fluctuating less than every construction in \S\ref{sec:calibrated}. The
deep arithmetic-only run sits systematically at or just below
$0.650919$, a pattern the data here cannot yet distinguish from a
slowly converging extrapolation.
